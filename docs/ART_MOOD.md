# WHERE IS DADA? — mood brief for the art

Read this before drawing anything. It says how the game should *feel*.
Each job's own prompt (in ART_PROMPTS.md) says how to draw that sprite,
and **if the two ever disagree, the job's prompt wins.**

## The game in one line

1993. A two-year-old in a green dragon costume climbs a corporate tower,
floor by floor, to bring his dad home from a job that has swallowed him.

## The feel

- **Funny first, angry underneath.** It's a toddler's tantrum against
  capitalism: slapstick on top, real anger about work, money and lost
  time with family underneath. Never preachy, never grim.
- **A 16-bit console game from 1993.** Chunky pixels, bold outlines,
  clear shapes, and a bit of cartoon exaggeration in poses and faces.
- **Warm heroes, tired office.** Everything that loves Tero is warm and
  soft. Everything that belongs to the company is grey, flat and worn out.

## The heroes: warm, round, alive

- **Tero** is a real human toddler (big head, short limbs, waddly) in a
  homemade-looking dragon costume. Brave, stubborn, cute without trying.
- **Dad** wears the same costume, faded and greyed by six months at the
  office. He looks tired, not evil: the costume is the last bit of him
  that's still his.
- **Elvis**, the office dog, is pure joy: scruffy, floppy and loyal.
- Saturated, cheerful colours, rounded shapes, and expressive eyes.

## The office: the world Tero fights through

- Beige, grey, olive and fluorescent white. Carpet tiles, cubicle walls,
  water coolers, motivational posters, piles of paper, red tape.
- Each floor has its own look: the basement mailroom, a cubicle farm,
  the boardroom, Legal's law books, an R&D lab, a security floor, a
  plush executive wing, and a cold, golden penthouse at the top.
- The background is muted on purpose, so the characters stand out.

## The enemies: sad and absurd, not scary

- Office workers the job has slowly turned into things: grey zombie
  clerks, pigs in suits, rats, robots, vampires, a gorilla, a snapping
  office plant. All still in their work clothes, all a bit pathetic.
- They should look **trapped, not evil**: Tero doesn't kill them, he
  sends them home. A freed worker should look relieved.
- Readable at a glance: one clear silhouette and one strong colour each.

## The bosses: the only real menace

- **Chad from Talent Acquisition** (a pig in a slim-fit suit): a smug,
  smiling salesman. Creepy because he never stops smiling.
- **Mr. Halvorsen**: the manager with a slideshow. Cold and controlling.
- **The Board**: five heads on one body at the top of the tower. Greedy
  and grotesque, the whole system in one monster.
- Bigger, darker and more detailed than the regular enemies.

## Period details: keep it 1993

- **Yes:** beige computers, CRT monitors, fax machines, desk phones with
  cords, floppy disks, VHS tapes, overhead projectors, pagers, boxy
  CCTV cameras, paper everywhere.
- **No:** smartphones, flat screens, laptops that look modern, touch
  screens, social media, or any real company logos or brand names.

## Readability rules (gameplay first)

- Sprites must read clearly against the muted backgrounds: a dark
  outline, clear silhouettes, and no fine detail that turns to noise.
- **Pickups always pop**: Dad's things, coins and the parachute are
  bright and glowing, so the player spots them instantly.
- Characters face **right**, with their feet on the bottom edge of the
  frame.

## Never

- No gore, blood or realistic violence. Fire is cartoony baby fire.
- No text or letters inside sprites, unless a prompt asks for it.
- No anime or chibi style, no 3D rendering, no painterly or blurred
  look, no gradients.
- No backgrounds, floors or shadows behind sprites: transparent only.
- Nothing that looks like a real person, character or brand.
